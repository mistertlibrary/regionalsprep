Start Here

  • Regionals run at West Essex from November 30 to December 2.
  • Almost every Regionals event is a 50-minute, 100-question multiple-choice test.
  • The top eight scores for most events in each of NJ FBLA's four regions advance to the State Leadership Conference.
  • Your score depends on how well you understand your event's knowledge areas. There are no shortcuts!

Know Your Test

Most FBLA tests are built from a study guide. The guide names the knowledge areas the test covers and (usually) cites how many of the 100 questions come from each. Learn about your event below!

    3

    Study Plan

    • Take a practice test first. Log in to FBLA Connect and select More, then Courses. Your score will reveal areas of strength and weakness.
    • Time yourself! One hundred questions in 50 minutes gives you 30 seconds per question.
    • Close your notes and focus on recall. Write down everything you know about one knowledge area, then check what you missed. (This is called retrieval practice; tail-wagging is optional.)
    • Sort your errors by knowledge area; address what's weakest first.
    • Study a little every day between now and your test. Short, spaced sessions beat one long cram.
    • Mix knowledge areas within a session instead of finishing one before you start the next.
    • Ask a teacher who teaches your subject or turn to Mr. Thompson for help locating resources.
    • Sleep well the night before!

    4

    Weighing Advice

    You'll find oodles of FBLA advice online. Some of it is sound, some is out of date, and some is downright unethical. Exercise the same sort of skepticism you would toward any other source.

    Is it current?

    Older guides from students and chapters describe a 60-minute test and say the last 10 questions break ties. Both statements are mistaken. Trust this year's guidelines over ancient advice.

    Does it match your study guide?

    Practice sets on Quizlet and similar sites vary in age and accuracy. Use them only after you've checked their topics against your event's knowledge areas. Same goes for prompting an AI tool for help.

    Is it honest?

    FBLA's Honor Code forbids competitors from saving competitive event or test materials. A study set rebuilt from someone's memory of a real test breaks the spirit of that rule. Don't tacitly endorse somebody else's loose morals.

    Is it free?

    Please don't buy anything! FBLA's sample tests come with your membership; our library databases are already paid for.

    6

    Test Day

    Before

    • No notes or study materials. Phones, smart watches, and headphones stay off and out of sight.
    • Bring a pencil. Scratch paper is allowed and is collected at the end.
    • Team events: each member tests alone and the scores are averaged. No collaboration.

    During

    • Answer every question. Wrong answers cost you nothing.
    • Check the clock at question 50. You want about 25 minutes left.
    • If a question stalls you, make your best guess and move on.
    • Read every option before you choose.
